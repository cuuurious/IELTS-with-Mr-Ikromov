Lesson 5 · Verb patterns

What you will learn

The infinitive is the basic form of the verb. In English it appears in two shapes — with to and without — and it can also have tenses. By the end of this lesson you will know when each shape is needed, how to talk about earlier and ongoing actions with the infinitive, and how to say who does the action.

ShapePatternExample
to-infinitiveto + verbShe wants to finish her work.
bare infinitiveverb (no to)She must work overtime.
negativenot to + verb / not + bare verbHe decided not to accept the job. She may not come.
🧩
In plain wordsThe infinitive is a verb waiting for a job. Another word (a verb, an adjective, a question word) decides whether it needs to. Learn the “job-giver” and you know the shape.
IELTS tipInfinitives of purpose (to reduce pollution, in order to save time) are everywhere in Task 2 and Task 1 answers. They are a simple way to show complex sentences.

Part 1

The to-infinitive: uses

Eleven situations where we use to + verb.

Uses

core learn these first · less common learn these second

a

To express purposecore

Does it answer “Why?” or “What for?”
  • She went to the bank to get some money.
  • The company is investing in solar panels to reduce its energy costs.
b

After certain verbscore

Is the main verb one of: advise, agree, appear, ask, decide, expect, hope, manage, offer, plan, promise, refuse, seem, want, afford, pretend, learn, fail, tend?
  • He advised me to apply for the job.
  • She managed to persuade the committee.
  • They refused to comment.
c

After question wordscore

Do you have know / decide / ask / learn / wonder / explain / want to know + who, what, where, how, which?
  • I can't decide where to go.
  • He explained how to operate the machine.
  • BUT why is followed by a subject + verb: I want to know why you've decided to leave.
d

After adjectivescore

Does the adjective show a feeling (glad, sorry, happy, afraid, ashamed, annoyed), willingness (willing, eager, reluctant, anxious) or character (clever, mean, kind)? Also lucky, fortunate.
  • He is glad to be back.
  • I was annoyed to hear that he had left.
  • He is reluctant to help.
  • It was nice of him to help. (character: It + be + adjective + of + noun/pronoun + to)
e

After too and enoughcore

Is it more / as much as needed or wanted? (Lesson 7)
  • She is too shy to talk to the manager.
  • We've got enough money to buy a new car.
f

After it + be + adjective / nouncore

Does a general statement begin with It is…?
  • It is important to get there on time.
  • It is her ambition to open her own shop.
g

After would like, would love, would prefercore

Do you mean a specific preference?
  • I would like to learn a foreign language.
h

After nouns and pronouns such as something, somewhere, anyone, nothingcore

Does it show something necessary or possible?
  • We've got a lot of homework to do.
  • Take something to drink on the bus.
i

An unexpected, often unpleasant result: only toless common

Does a surprise follow the action?
  • He rushed to the back door only to discover that it was locked.
  • She came home to find her sister waiting.
j

After the first, second, next, last, best + noun / adjectivecore

Is it a place in a sequence?
  • He was the first to arrive.
  • Simon was the last person to arrive.

More verbs followed by a to-infinitive

Verbs that are followed directly by to: afford · agree · appear · arrange · attempt · choose · decide · expect · fail · hope · learn · manage · offer · plan · pretend · promise · refuse · seem · tend · threaten · want · wish.

Verbs that take an object, then to: advise · allow · ask · beg · encourage · expect · force · invite · order · persuade · remind · teach · tell · want · warn. The lawyer advised me to accept the offer.

Two small rules

  • Negative: put not before to: He decided not to accept the job.
  • If two to-infinitives are joined by and / or, the second to can be dropped: He agreed to come and help us.

Typical mistakes

I want that you help me.
I want you to help me.
She went to the bank for to get money.
She went to the bank to get money.
I don't know why to go.
I don't know why I should go.
Part 2

The bare infinitive (without to)

Five places where the infinitive has no to.

Uses

core learn these first · less common learn these second

a

After modal verbscore

Is it can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would?
  • You must study hard.
  • She may not come. (BUT ought takes to: She ought to find a job.)
b

After let, make, see, hear, feel (+ object)core

Does a verb of the senses or let / make come before the object?
  • They made him pay for the damage.
  • I heard Jack talk on the phone. (the complete action)
  • They saw her talk to the manager.
c

After had better and would rathercore

Is it a strong suggestion or a preference?
  • You had better sign the contract.
  • I would rather go home now.
d

help: with or without tocore

help + (object) + to or bare infinitive
  • She helped me (to) fix the tap.
🔁
The passive changes itbe made / be heard / be seen take to: He was made to pay for the damage. She was seen to talk to the manager. But when see, hear, watch are followed by an -ing form, nothing changes: I saw her getting into a taxi → She was seen getting into a taxi.
modal + baremust go, can swim
let / make + object + barelet me go, made him pay
had better / would rather + bareyou had better go

Typical mistakes

He must to study.
He must study.
She let me to go.
She let me go.
He was made pay.
He was made to pay.
Part 3

Tenses of the infinitive

The infinitive has four tenses in the active and two in the passive.

ActivePassive
present(to) give(to) be given
present continuous(to) be giving—
perfect(to) have given(to) have been given
perfect continuous(to) have been giving—

What each one means

InfinitiveRefers toExample
present: (to) givenow or the futureShe wants to talk to him now. Sandra wants to move next year.
present continuous: (to) be givingan action happening nowHe is believed to be hiding in the mountains.
perfect: (to) have givenearlier than the main verbShe claims to have met Richard Gere. (first she met him, then she claimed)
perfect continuous: (to) have been givingearlier, with emphasis on durationShe claims to have been working hard lately.
passive present: (to) be givennow or futureHe hopes to be given a pay rise soon.
passive perfect: (to) have been givenearlierHe is said to have been injured in an accident.
🔎
Where you find themThe perfect infinitive often follows seem, appear, believe, know, claim, expect, say and modal verbs: He must have left. She seems to have grown taller.

Verb tenses and the infinitive

The finite verbThe infinitive
he goes / will goto go
he is going / will be goingto be going

he was going / has been going / had been going / will have been goingto have been going
IELTS tipReporting claims in academic writing uses these forms: The company is reported to have lost millions; the minister is said to be preparing a statement. They show high-level grammar range.
Part 4

The subject of the infinitive

When the infinitive has its own subject.

Same verb, two ideas:

She wants to leave now.same subject: she wants, she leaves — no subject before to
She wants them to leave now.different subjects: she wants, they leave — object pronoun before to

Rule

  • The subject of the infinitive is omitted when it is the same as the subject of the main verb: She wants to leave.
  • The subject is not omitted when it is different. It comes before the infinitive and can be an object pronoun (me, you, him, her, us, them), a name (Kate) or a noun (the woman): She wants them to leave. I want Kate to explain this.
Same subjectDifferent subject
They all want to answer the question.The teacher wants them to answer the question.
I must wash the car.I want him to wash the car.
You mustn't be late.I don't want you to be late.

Typical mistakes

I want that he comes.
I want him to come.
She wants that we leave.
She wants us to leave.

Review: mixed infinitive

PatternExample
It is + adjective + toSpending a lot of money on clothes is foolish. → It is foolish to spend a lot of money on clothes.
made → were made toThe teacher made us learn it. → We were made to learn it.
not allowed toEating is not allowed. → Nobody is allowed to eat.
