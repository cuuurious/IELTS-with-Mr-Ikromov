Lesson 4 · Tenses

What you will learn

Lessons 1–3 gave you the building blocks: present, past and future forms. This lesson is about choosing. Most grammar mistakes in IELTS Speaking and Writing are not wrong forms — they are the right form in the wrong place. You will learn the signals (time words, results, duration) that tell you which tense to use.

TimeSimpleContinuousPerfectPerfect continuous
PresentI workI am workingI have workedI have been working
PastI workedI was workingI had workedI had been working
FutureI will workI will be workingI will have workedI will have been working
🗺️
In plain wordsRead the table like a map. Rows = when (present, past, future). Columns = how you see the action: as a whole (simple), in progress (continuous), finished before a point (perfect), or lasting up to a point (perfect continuous).
Question to askChoose
Is it finished and the time is over / stated?past simple
Does it connect past to now (result, experience, unfinished time)?present perfect
Does it stress how long, up to now (for / since)?present perfect continuous
Was something earlier than another past event?past perfect
Is it in progress at the time you are talking about?continuous
IELTS tipExaminers reward accurate range: a Speaking answer that moves correctly between present perfect (I've been studying English for six years), past simple (I started when I was ten) and future (I'm going to take the exam in May) sounds band 7+.

Part 1

Past simple or present perfect?

The most important contrast in English tenses.

Same verb, two ideas:

Simon Cook painted his first picture in 1980.past simple: the time is stated (when? in 1980)
Simon Cook has painted a lot of pictures.present perfect: the time is not stated or not important

The big difference

Past simplePresent perfect
a finished action at a definite time (stated, known or implied)an action at an unstated time, when the exact time is neither mentioned nor implied
not related to the presentrelated to the present (a result, an experience)
finished period: yesterday, last year, in 1998, agounfinished period: today, this week, so far, since, for, ever, never, yet, already, just
an action that began and finished in the past: George was a basketball player for ten years. (now a coach)an action that started in the past and continues up to now: Jim has worked as a waiter for fifteen years. (still a waiter)

More details

  • People who are no longer alive: always past simple, even without a date: Charles Dickens wrote “Oliver Twist”.
  • Unfinished vs finished time: I wrote three letters this morning (it is evening; the morning is over) vs I've written three letters this morning (it is still morning).
  • An action that cannot be repeated: Mr Clark taught Maths for thirty years (he has retired); but Mrs Nelson has taught French for twenty years (she still does).
  • News + details: announce news with the present perfect, then give details in the past simple: I've just seen the new boss. I was talking to Carol when he came in.

Typical mistakes

I have seen him yesterday.
I saw him yesterday.
When have you arrived?
When did you arrive?
I have read this book a month ago.
I read this book a month ago.

Part 3

Perfect simple or perfect continuous?

Same time, different focus: the result or the activity.

Same verb, two ideas:

I've read your report.present perfect: finished, I can talk about the result
I've been reading your report.present perfect continuous: activity up to now; maybe not finished

Choosing

Present perfect simplePresent perfect continuous
result, number, “how many”: She has written ten pages.activity, duration, “how long”: She has been writing all morning.
state verbs: I have known him for years.not with state verbs
finished or completed: I've painted the fence.not necessarily finished; stress on the activity: I've been painting the fence. (paint on my hands)

The same logic in the past

Replace have / has with had and you get the past equivalents.

NowPast
He has broken his leg, so he can't walk.He had broken his leg, so he couldn't walk.
He has been waiting for hours, so he is bored.He had been waiting for hours, so he was bored.

Typical mistakes

I have been knowing him for years.
I have known him for years.
She has been writing five emails.
She has written five emails.
How long do you live here?
How long have you lived here?
Part 4

All the tenses together

Now mix present, past and future in connected texts.

📝
Reading a textUnderline every time word first: now, last year, since 2019, next week, by the time. Each one tells you the tense. Then decide the tense of every gap.

Review: the uses of the present forms

FormMain uses
present simplehabits, permanent situations, general truths, timetables
present continuousnow, temporary situations, changing situations, fixed arrangements, annoying habits with always
present perfectexperience, result now, unfinished time (for / since), news
present perfect continuousduration up to now, a recent activity with a visible result

Review: the uses of the past forms

FormMain uses
past simplefinished actions, steps in a story, finished habits, people no longer alive
past continuousin progress at a past time, interrupted actions, background, simultaneous actions
past perfectearlier than another past event, a result visible in the past
past perfect continuousduration before a past event, an activity that explains a past result
used to / wouldfinished past habits (would: not for states)

Review: the uses of the future forms

FormMain uses
willpredictions (opinion), decisions now, offers, promises, threats, warnings, requests, hopes
be going toplans, intentions; predictions with evidence
present continuousfixed arrangements
present simpletimetables and programmes
future continuousin progress at a future time; routines; polite questions
future perfect (continuous)finished by / duration up to a future time
Part 5

Mistakes and rewriting

The sentence-transformation patterns from the exam, and the mistakes behind them.

PatternExample
since + past simpleWhen did they graduate? → How long is it since they graduated?
agoHow long ago did they buy their tickets?
first time + present perfectI've never eaten Chinese food. → It's the first time I've eaten Chinese food.
ever with superlativesIt's the saddest story I've ever heard.
stillShe hasn't finished yet. → She is still decorating.
last timeWe haven't been for a year. → The last time we went was a year ago.
Part 6

Speaking: the mission to Saturn

Work in pairs. First speak, then (if you like) write down your best sentences.

Task 1 · What have they arranged? present continuous

Dr Samson and his team are going to Saturn: leave Earth on Saturday · arrive on Saturn next Tuesday · return to Earth in two months. Say what they are doing.

They're leaving Earth on Saturday.

Task 2 · What will they be doing / have done? future continuous / future perfect

While they are there: do experiments · collect samples · search for life. By the time they come back: take 3,000 photographs · walk on the surface · be in space for two months.

They'll be doing experiments while they're there. They'll have taken more than 3,000 photographs.

Talk to your partner: How long have you been learning English? When did you start? What are you doing now? What are you going to do next?
