Lesson 20 · Emphasis and inversion

What you will learn

Two ways to make sentences stronger and more formal: emphasis (It was Lydia who…, What I need is…) and inversion (Never have I…, Not only did they…, Only then did we…). Both are very useful for IELTS Writing and Speaking.

StructureExample
It is / was … who / thatIt was Steven Spielberg who directed E.T.
What … is · All (that) …What I need is a holiday. · All she cares about is money.
do / does / didShe did promise to call.
question word + everWhere ever did you find this map?
negative adverbial + inversionNever have I seen… · No sooner had she… than…
so / neither / norSo do I. · Neither can I.
inverted conditionalsHad I known… · Were I you… · Should he call…
place / movement + inversionDown came the rain. · Here comes the bride.
🔊
In plain wordsEmphasis = “this is the important part”. Inversion = the auxiliary comes before the subject, usually after a negative or limiting word at the start.
IELTS tipIn Writing Task 2 one or two inversions raise the grammar score: Not only does exercise improve health, but it also reduces stress. · Only by investing in education can a country prosper. Do not overuse them.

Part 1

Emphasis

Put the spotlight on one part of the sentence.

Five ways

core learn these first · less common learn these second

a

It is / was + emphasised part + who / thatcore

Do you want to stress the subject, the object or the time / place?
  • It was Sheila who was planting flowers. (subject: who / that)
  • It is flowers that Sheila is planting. (object: that)
  • It is in the garden that Sheila is planting flowers. (adverbial: that)
  • It wasn't the dog's barking that woke me up, it was the alarm clock. (contrast)
b

What … is / was · … is what …core

Do you want to stress the whole idea or the verb?
  • What I need is a holiday. · A holiday is what I need.
  • What keeps me in shape is jogging.
  • What Greg did was (to) update the files.
c

All (that) … = the only thingcore

Is it the only thing?
  • All (that) she cares about is money.
  • All (that) he did was call me to say goodbye.
d

do / does / did + bare infinitivecore

Do you want to confirm or contrast something?
  • She does believe in supernatural powers.
  • He did go to the reception.
  • Do stay a little longer. (imperative: polite persuasion)
e

question word + ever · ever in negativesless common

Are you surprised?
  • Who ever told you I was getting married?
  • Where ever did you find this old map? (NOT which / whose)
  • Nobody ever explained this to me.
⚠️
RememberUse who / that for the subject. Use that for the object and adverbial. After What … is, the verb after is is usually (to) + base.

Typical mistakes

It was Sheila what was planting flowers.
It was Sheila who / that was planting flowers.
What I need are a holiday.
What I need is a holiday.
She does believes in it.
She does believe in it.
Part 2

Contrast and surprise

Not this, but that.

Contrast

Use It's not … that …, it's … to correct or contrast: It's not a pencil that I need, it's a pen.

do / does / did and ever

Short and strong: I did tell you! · He does work hard. · Whatever have you done?

Typical mistakes

It's not Sam that are a policeman.
It's not Sam who is a policeman, it's Daniel.
Whichever told you that?
Who ever told you that?
Part 3

Inversion

Auxiliary before the subject.

1) auxiliary / modal + subject + main verb

After…Examples
Seldom, Rarely, Little, Barely, Never (before), Nowhere (else), Not (even) onceNever have I seen such a beautiful woman. · Seldom do we go out since the baby was born.
Hardly (ever) … when / No sooner … thanNo sooner had she fallen asleep than the phone rang.
Not only … but (also)Not only did they make a donation, but they also promised to build a shelter.
Only by, Only then, Only after / if / when, Not until / till, Not sinceOnly then did we realise… · Not until midnight did he arrive.
In no way, On no account, In / Under no circumstancesUnder no circumstances should you enter this room.

2) main verb + subject

After a place or movement word at the start, when the subject is a noun: Outside the house was a sports car. · On the sofa sat an old man. · Down came the rain. · Here comes the bride. If the subject is a pronoun there is no inversion: Here she comes. · Up you get.

In direct speech with a noun subject: ‘I don't like this hotel,’ said Henry.

⚠️
No inversionWe seldom go out — no inversion, because seldom is not at the beginning of the sentence.

Typical mistakes

Never I have seen it.
Never have I seen it.
No sooner she had left than…
No sooner had she left than…
Not only he is rich but…
Not only is he rich but…
Part 4

So / neither and inverted conditionals

Agreement and formal if-clauses.

so / neither / nor / as

UseExample
agree with an affirmative‘I love chocolate ice cream.’ ‘So do I.’
agree with a negative‘I can't stand violent films.’ ‘Neither / Nor can I.’
as + auxiliary + subjectShe was a talented musician, as was her sister.

Inverted conditionals (without if)

TypePatternExample
1Should + subjectShould he call, tell him I'm out.
2Were + subjectWere I you, I wouldn't trust her.
3Had + subject + past participleHad I been told, I would have offered my help.

Typical mistakes

Neither I can.
Neither can I.
So I do.
So do I.
Was I you…
Were I you…

Speaking: make it stronger
