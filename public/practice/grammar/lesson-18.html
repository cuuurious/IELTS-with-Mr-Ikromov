Lesson 18 · Reported speech

What you will learn

We report what people said in two ways: direct speech (the exact words, with quotation marks) and reported speech (the meaning, no quotation marks). This lesson covers statements.

You will learnExample
say / tellHe told me that he was cold.
tense changes (backshift)I'm tired → She said she was tired.
time and place wordstomorrow → the next day · here → there
pronounsI → he / she · my → his / her
modalswill → would · can → could
when tenses stay the samepresent reporting verb · general truths · conditionals
💬
In plain wordsReported speech = the meaning, told later. Time moves forward, so the tense moves back.
IELTS tipReported speech helps in Speaking Part 1 and 3 (My teacher once told me that…) and in Writing Task 1 and 2 (The report states that…). Use say / tell correctly.

Part 1

say and tell

Two small verbs, two different patterns.

Patterns

core learn these first · less common learn these second

a

say (+ to + person) + (that) clausecore

Is there a person after the verb?
  • He said (that) he was cold.
  • He said to me (that) he was cold.
  • He said, ‘I'm cold.’ (direct)
b

tell + person (always) + (that) clausecore

Do you name the listener?
  • He told me (that) he was cold.
  • He told me, ‘I'm cold.’ (never "told that")
c

fixed expressionscore

Is it a collocation?
  • say: say hello / goodbye / good morning, say something / nothing, say a prayer, say so, say a few words
  • tell: tell the truth, tell a lie, tell a secret, tell a story, tell a joke, tell the time, tell the difference, tell one's name

Same verb, two ideas:

She said she was tired.no person named
She told me she was tired.person named (me)

Typical mistakes

She said me that she was ill.
She told me / She said to me that she was ill.
He told that he was busy.
He said that he was busy. / He told us…
I can't say the difference.
I can't tell the difference.
Part 2

How tenses change

The tense moves back when the reporting verb is in the past.

Backshift

DirectReportedExample
present simplepast simple‘I need a car.’ → He said he needed a car.
present continuouspast continuous‘He's sleeping.’ → She said he was sleeping.
present perfectpast perfect‘I've seen it.’ → He said he had seen it.
past simplepast simple / past perfect‘I got up late.’ → She said she (had) got up late.
willwould‘I'll be able to…’ → He said he would be able to…
cancould‘I can speak French.’ → He said he could speak French.
maymight‘I may call you.’ → He said he might call me.
mustmust / had to‘You must finish.’ → He said I must / had to finish.
needn'tneedn't / didn't need to / didn't have to‘You needn't pay.’ → He said I didn't need to pay.
shallshould (advice) · offer to (offer)‘Shall I help?’ → He offered to help.

Typical mistakes

She said she is moving tomorrow.
She said she was moving the next day.
He told me he will come.
He told me he would come.
He said I am late.
He said I was late.
Part 3

When do tenses not change?

Sometimes you do not need to move the tense back.

Tenses can stay the same when…

  • the reporting verb is present (says, has said, will say): Mum says dinner is ready.
  • passing on a message: Debbie says she needs the car tonight.
  • reading a letter, article or notice: The article says people use too much water.
  • we refer to something said very often: Mum often says I shouldn't talk to strangers.
  • up-to-date reporting (what was said is still true): The boy said he is lost.
  • a general truth: The teacher said Paris is / was the capital of France.
  • second and third conditionals: Eric said if he had more time, he would take up a hobby.
🧠
Ask yourselfIs it old news or fresh? Is it still true? If yes, you may keep the tense.

Typical mistakes

He said if he has more time, he will…
He said if he had more time, he would… (conditional type 2: keep)
The article said people use too much water. (reading it now)
The article says people use too much water.
