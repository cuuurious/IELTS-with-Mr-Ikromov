Lesson 9 · Modal verbs

What you will learn

This lesson covers ability (can, could, be able to, manage to), permission (can, may, could, might, be allowed to), polite requests, offers and suggestions (shall, will, would you mind, let's). These are the everyday modals of speaking.

FunctionExamples
abilitycan · could · was able to · managed to
permissionCan / May / Could I…? · You may… · was allowed to
requestsCan / Could / Will / Would you…? · Would you mind + -ing?
offersShall I…? · I'll… · Would you like me to…?
suggestionsShall we…? · We could… · Why don't we…? · How about + -ing?
🗣️
In plain wordsThese forms are about people and politeness: the longer and softer the structure, the more polite you sound.
IELTS tipIn Speaking Part 1 use “could” and “be able to” for past ability, and “would” for polite or hypothetical answers: I was able to speak English fluently after two years.

Part 1

Ability: can, could, be able to

Present, past, and one-off successes.

Rules

core learn these first · less common learn these second

a

can — ability nowcore

Can he do it now?
  • Terry can drive a car.
  • Sue can't dance.
b

could / was able to — general ability in the pastcore

Was it a habit or skill over time?
  • When he was ten, he could / was able to ride a bicycle.
  • He couldn't / wasn't able to play chess when he was younger.
c

was able to / managed to — one successful eventcore

Did he succeed once, in a specific situation?
  • Paula was able to climb to the top of the mountain.
  • Although he felt ill, he managed to finish the paperwork.
d

couldn't / wasn't able to — one failed eventcore

Did it fail once?
  • She couldn't / wasn't able to finish the book.
  • I couldn't open the door.

be able to in other tenses

Can has only two forms: can (present) and could (past). For all other tenses use be able to.

TenseForm
present simplecan / am, is, are able to
past simplecould / was, were able to
present perfecthave, has been able to
past perfecthad been able to
future simplewill be able to
future perfectwill have been able to
infinitive / -ingto be able to / being able to

Same verb, two ideas:

I could swim when I was five.general ability
I was able to swim across the lake yesterday.one specific success

Typical mistakes

I can to swim.
I can swim.
I'll can help you.
I'll be able to help you.
Yesterday I could pass the test.
Yesterday I was able to / managed to pass the test.
Part 2

Permission

Asking for, giving and refusing permission.

Asking for permission

FormRegister
Can I…?informal
Could / May I…?polite
Might I…?very formal; rare
Do / Would you mind if I…?polite

Reply: Certainly. · Of course. · Why not? · I'm afraid you can't.

Giving and refusing

FunctionForm
givingcan / may: You can use my phone. · You may make a call here. (more formal)
refusingcan't / mustn't / may not: You can't stay. · Customers may not enter. (notice)

Do not use could or might to give permission: “Could I borrow your dictionary?” → “Yes, you can.” (not Yes, you could)

Permission in the past and rules

  • General permission in the past: could or was / were allowed to. I could / was allowed to go to parties when I was young.
  • One particular occasion: only was / were allowed to. I was allowed to go to John's party last night.
  • Rules and laws: can or be allowed to. All citizens over 18 can / are allowed to vote.

Same verb, two ideas:

May I use your phone?asks you personally to allow me
Are we allowed to use the office phone?asks what the rule is
Part 3

Requests, offers and suggestions

Be polite and natural.

Requests

FormExample
Can / Will you…? (informal)Can you get me a glass of water?
Could / Would you…? (polite)Could you type these letters, please?
Would you mind + -ing?Would you mind closing the window?

Reply: Yes, I'd be happy to. · Certainly. · Of course. · I'm sorry, but I can't.

Offers

FormExample
Shall I / we…?Shall I explain it again?
Can / Could I…? = Would you like me to…?Can I give you a hand?
I'll… (willing, informal)You look tired. I'll do the ironing.

Suggestions

FormExample
Shall we…?Shall we go to the theatre?
Let's…Let's watch a video.
We can / could…We could have the meeting on Tuesday.
Why don't we…? / How about / What about + -ing?How about going for a walk?
IELTS tipIn Speaking Part 3 use “could” to suggest solutions: Governments could invest more in public transport.

Typical mistakes

Would you mind to open the door?
Would you mind opening the door?
Shall you help me?
Will / Could you help me? (request) · Shall I help you? (offer)

Part 5

Speaking: polite language

Work in pairs. First speak, then (if you like) write.

Task 1 · Role play requests / offers

Role play: in a hotel, in a shop, at a doctor's. Practise Could you…?, Would you mind…?, Shall I…?

Task 2 · I managed to… ability

Tell your partner about something difficult you managed to do. Use managed to, was able to and couldn't.

Task 3 · Plan a day suggestions

Plan a day out together. Use Shall we…?, How about…?, We could…, Why don't we…?
