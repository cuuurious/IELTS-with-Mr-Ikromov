What you will learn

This lesson covers advice (should, ought to, had better), possibility and deduction (may, might, could, must, can't) and perfect modals (must have done, can't have done, should have done, could have done).

MeaningPresentPast
advice / criticismshould · ought to · had bettershould have + pp · ought to have + pp
sure (true)must + infinitivemust have + pp
sure (not true)can't + infinitivecan't have + pp

📈
In plain wordsImagine a certainty scale: must (100%) → should / probably (80%) → may / might / could (30–50%) → can't (0%).
IELTS tipIn Speaking Part 3 and Writing Task 2 use hedging modals to speculate: This might lead to…, It could be argued that…, Governments should… They show a high level of control.

Part 1

Advice: should, ought to, had better

Recommend, warn and criticise.

Forms

core learn these first · less common learn these second

a

should / ought to + infinitive — I advise youcore

Is it a good idea?
  • It's late. You should go home.
  • We ought to respect the elderly. (ought is followed by to)
b

had better + infinitive — advice with a warningcore

Will something bad happen if you don't?
  • You had better take that seriously, or it will get worse.
  • You had better not be late. (no "to" after better)
c

must — very strong advice or an ordercore

Is it almost an order?
  • “You must follow a healthy diet,” the doctor said. (an order, likely to be obeyed)
  • My friend said: “You should follow a healthy diet.” (advice, may or may not be followed)

Same verb, two ideas:

You should see a doctor.advice: you may ignore it
You must see a doctor.a strong order: do it now

Typical mistakes

You should to go.
You should go.
You had better to leave.
You had better leave.
You would better go.
You had better go.
You ought go.
You ought to go.
Part 2

Possibility and logical deduction

How sure are you?

Present and future

MeaningModalExample
I'm sure it is truemustShe must know him well. | = I'm sure she knows him well
I'm sure it isn't truecan'tHe can't be at work. His car is here.
perhaps / it is possiblemay / might / couldHe may / might / could be late.
continuous situationmodal + be + -ingShe must be working tomorrow. He could be working late.
⚠️
Do not sayNever use mustn't for a negative deduction: it means “forbidden”. Say can't: He can't be at home.

Be likely to

Be likely to = probably. It is stronger than may and weaker than should: The Austrian driver is likely to win. It is likely that the driver will win.

Typical mistakes

She mustn't be at home.
She can't be at home.
He must to be tired.
He must be tired.
Part 3

Perfect modals: the past

Deduction, criticism and what could have happened.

Form: modal + have + past participle

MeaningFormExample
I'm sure it happenedmust have + ppHe must have been sleeping.
I'm sure it didn't happencan't have + ppHe can't have known the truth.
perhaps it happenedmay / might / could have + ppThey might have missed the bus.
it was a good idea, but you didn't (criticism)should / ought to have + ppYou should have come to me for help.
it was a mistake, but you didshouldn't / ought not to have + ppThe owners shouldn't have left the window unlocked.
it was possible, but it didn't happencould have + ppThe driver could have been killed. (He wasn't.)

Same verb, two ideas:

You should have told me.criticism: you didn't tell me
You must have told her.deduction: I'm sure you told her
⏪
In plain wordsPast modals look back. Ask: “Am I guessing (must have / might have)? Am I criticising (should have)? Or am I saying it nearly happened (could have)?”

Typical mistakes

He must have went.
He must have gone.
She should of called.
She should have called.
They mustn't have known.
They can't have known.
IELTS tipIn Speaking Part 2 use could have and might have to show what else could have happened: If I hadn't gone, I might have missed a great chance.

Part 5

Speaking: speculate and advise

Work in pairs. First speak, then (if you like) write.

Task 1 · Detective must / can't / might

Look at a photo of a stranger or a messy room. Guess what happened: He must have…, She can't have…, They might have…

Task 2 · Agony aunt should / had better

Your partner has a problem (cannot sleep, argues with a colleague, wants to learn English fast). Give advice: should, ought to, had better.

Task 3 · I wish I had should have

Tell your partner two things you should have done differently in the last year.
