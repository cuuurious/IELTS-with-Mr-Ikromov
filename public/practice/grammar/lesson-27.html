Lesson 27 · Quantifiers

What you will learn

Quantifiers tell us how many or how much. They are very frequent in Writing Task 1 and Task 2: a large number of, a great deal of, few, little, both, neither. The choice depends on whether the noun is countable or uncountable.

You will learnExample
much / many / a lot ofHow much milk? How many eggs? a lot of work
too much / too manytoo much salt · too many books
a few / few · a little / littlea few eggs (enough) · very few eggs (not enough)
both / neither / eitherBoth of them are… · Neither of them is…
all / noneAll of them are… · None of them is…
both … and · neither … nor · either … orNeither Daniel nor Mark drinks coffee.
📏
In plain wordsCount it? many / few / a few. Can't count it? much / little / a little. Two things? both / neither / either. More than two? all / none.
IELTS tipIn Task 1: The number of students rose… vs The amount of money spent…. Few / little are negative in meaning (few people agree = almost nobody). A few / a little are positive.

Part 1

much, many, a lot of, too much, too many

Countable or uncountable?

Patterns

core learn these first · less common learn these second

a

a lot of / lots ofcore

Is it an affirmative sentence?
  • Mary has got a lot of dresses. There is a lot of coffee in the pot. (countable and uncountable)
  • Have you got many CDs? Yes, I've got a lot. (no noun: no of)
b

much (uncountable) · many (countable)core

Is it a question or a negative?
  • Is there much cheese? There isn't much cheese left.
  • Have you got many toys? I haven't got many toys.
c

how much / how manycore

Do you ask about amount or number?
  • How much milk do you take in your tea?
  • How many workers were fired?
d

too much / too manycore

Is it more than you want?
  • You've put too much salt in the soup.
  • She's got too many books. She doesn't know where to put them.

Typical mistakes

I haven't got many time.
I haven't got much time.
There are too much people.
There are too many people.
How many money?
How much money?
Part 2

a few / few · a little / little

Enough or not enough?

Meaning

CountableUncountableMeaning
positive: some, enougha few eggsa little sugarWe can make an omelette / a cake.
negative: hardly any(very) few eggs(very) little sugarWe can't make an omelette / a cake.

Jim has got a few stamps. He can post all the letters. Ben has got very few stamps. He can't post all of them.

Typical mistakes

He has few money.
He has little money.
She has a little friends.
She has a few friends.
Part 3

both, neither, either, all, none

Two things or more than two.

Two

WordMeaningExample
both (of)two, positive; plural verbBoth of them are typists. Both cats and dogs are affectionate pets.
neither (of)two, negativeNeither of them is / are married. Neither candidate is suitable.
either (of)one or the otherEither place is fine. I didn't buy either of them. John doesn't like coffee, and I don't either.

More than two

WordMeaningExample
all (of)more than two, positive; plural verbAll the students are present. All of them are young.
none (of)more than two, negativeNone of these shirts fits / fit me.

Position and pairs

  • both / all after be and auxiliary, before the main verb: They are both happy. They have all passed.
  • both … and + plural verb: Both Judy and Laura are British.
  • neither … nor / either … or + verb agrees with the nearer subject: Neither Daniel nor Mark drinks coffee. Either my parents or John's parents are going…

Typical mistakes

Neither Tom or Anne came.
Neither Tom nor Anne came.
I didn't like none of them.
I didn't like any of them. / I liked none of them.
